Cleaning schedule template you can copy and fill in

By Kerem Kaya, maker of Rocco

Last updated November 21, 2026

A cleaning schedule template is a table with a day, a focus area, the tasks, who does them and the minutes each one takes. Fill it in by sorting your jobs into daily, weekly and monthly lists, then giving each weekly job its own day so that no day takes more than 20 to 30 minutes. This page has a blank template, a seven-step way to fill it in and a worked example.

Key takeaways

  • A template has three parts: your tasks, how often each one is needed, and which day it lands on.
  • Start from frequency (daily, weekly, monthly), then spread the weekly jobs over the week instead of one day.
  • Leave one free day. A template with no slack gets abandoned in the first busy week.
  • Copy the blank table below into a document, a notes app or a spreadsheet, or print it.

How do I fill in a cleaning schedule template?

  1. List every task by frequency. Write three short lists: daily, weekly and monthly. N.C. Cooperative Extension sorts household jobs the same way: kitchen cleanup daily; bathroom, bedding, vacuuming, dusting, mopping and the fridge weekly; blankets, appliances and cabinets monthly.
  2. Estimate the minutes. Be honest and a little generous. Most single jobs land between 5 and 20 minutes.
  3. Spread the weekly jobs. The same extension author says the weekly jobs need not all happen on one day: choose one or two and add them to your daily routine. Put one weekly job on each day, with the longest job on the day you have the most time.
  4. Set a daily cap. Pick a number, such as 20 minutes on weekdays, and move a task if a day goes over it.
  5. Put the monthly jobs on a calendar. Give each one a fixed date, for example the first Sunday, so it does not live in your head.
  6. Name who does what. In a shared home, write a name in the Who column and rotate it every week. See how to split chores fairly.
  7. Test it for two weeks. Move any task you keep skipping to another day, or split it in two.

Which template format works best?

  • Paper on the fridge: good for shared homes, because everyone sees it.
  • Notes app or spreadsheet: easy to change and to share with a partner.
  • Calendar reminders: best for monthly and seasonal jobs.
  • A chore app: best when you want a reminder and proof that the task is done.

Questions people ask

How do I make a cleaning schedule?

List your tasks as daily, weekly and monthly, estimate the minutes for each, then spread the weekly jobs across the days so no day goes over your daily limit. Add one free day, put monthly jobs on fixed dates, and adjust after two weeks.

What should a cleaning schedule template include?

Day, focus area, tasks, who is responsible, estimated minutes and a done box. A separate short list for daily jobs and a calendar for monthly jobs keeps the weekly table readable.

How many tasks should I put on one day?

One focus area per day is enough for most homes. Keep weekday sessions around 10 to 20 minutes and put the longest job on the day you have the most time.

Can I use the same template in a shared home?

Yes. Add a Who column, give each person a zone and rotate the zones every week so nobody is stuck with the bathroom for good.
