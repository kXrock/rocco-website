How to split chores fairly with a partner or roommates

By Kerem Kaya, maker of Rocco

Last updated November 15, 2026

To split chores fairly, write down every chore in the home, estimate how many minutes each one takes per week, let each person pick the chores they mind least, and balance the total minutes rather than the number of chores. Then agree on what "done" looks like for each chore and hold a 5 minute check-in once a week to adjust.

Key takeaways

  • Fair means similar time, not the same number of tasks. Ten minutes of trash is not the same as an hour of laundry.
  • Include the invisible work: planning meals, buying supplies, noticing what is running out.
  • Let people pick by preference first. Someone who does not mind dishes should do the dishes.
  • Agree on "done" in plain words before anyone starts.
  • A short weekly check-in solves most chore fights before they start.

This matters to people. In Pew Research Center's 2016 Religious Landscape Study, 56% of married US adults said sharing household chores is very important to a successful marriage. The method below works the same for couples, roommates and families.

How do I divide chores fairly, step by step?

  1. List every chore. Walk through each room together and write down everything that has to happen in a normal week, including shopping, planning meals, paying shared bills and refilling soap or toilet paper.
  2. Estimate minutes per week. Next to each chore, write how often it happens and how long it takes. Dishes after dinner at 10 minutes a night is 70 minutes a week.
  3. Pick by preference. Take turns choosing chores, starting with the ones each person likes or minds least. Some people prefer cooking and others prefer cleaning up after.
  4. Balance by time, not by count. Add up each person's minutes. Move or trade chores until the totals are close. If one person works far longer hours outside the home, agree openly on a different ratio.
  5. Agree what "done" looks like. For each chore, write one plain sentence: "Dishes are done when the sink is empty and the counter is wiped." This prevents most arguments about quality.
  6. Hold a weekly 5 minute check-in. Same time each week, ask three questions: what worked, what did not, and does anything need to swap?

How do I split chores with roommates?

With roommates, keep personal things personal (your own room, your own dishes, your own laundry) and split only the shared spaces. Rotate the shared zones once a month so nobody owns the bathroom forever.

What should I do when someone does not do their part?

Assume a reason before a motive. People skip chores because they forgot, were tired, or understood "done" differently far more often than because they do not care. Talk about the chore, not the person, and bring a fix.

  • Bring it up once, at the weekly check-in or a calm moment, not in the middle of the mess.
  • Describe what happened, not who they are.
  • Ask if the chore still suits them. Maybe a swap fixes it.
  • Agree on one small change and try it for a week.

Some words that tend to land well:

"I noticed the trash went out late this week. Is Thursday still a good day for you, or should we swap it for something else?"

"I think we picture 'clean kitchen' differently. Can we write down what done looks like, so neither of us has to guess?"

"I am feeling stretched with my part right now. Can we look at the minutes again and see if it still adds up?"

If the same chore keeps getting missed, move it to a different time or person rather than repeating the same talk.

Questions people ask

What is the fairest way to split chores?

Balance total minutes per week, not the number of chores. List every chore with its weekly time, let each person pick what they mind least, then trade until the totals are close.

Should chores be split 50/50 between partners?

A 50/50 split of time is a good default when both partners have similar hours outside the home. If one partner works much longer hours, agree on a different ratio openly instead of letting it happen by default.

How often should roommates rotate chores?

Once a month works for most homes. It is long enough to learn the job and short enough that nobody is stuck with the bathroom for good.

What if my roommate never cleans?

Talk once, calmly, about the specific chore and ask whether a swap would help. Agree on what done looks like and try one change for a week. If nothing changes, involve everyone in the home at a shared check-in.
