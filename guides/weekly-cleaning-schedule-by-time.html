Weekly cleaning schedule for 15, 30 or 60 minutes a day

By Kerem Kaya, maker of Rocco

Last updated November 21, 2026

A weekly cleaning schedule works best when it starts from the minutes you really have. For a small home, 15 minutes a day covers the basics (about 1.5 hours a week). For a two-bedroom home, 30 minutes a day (about 2.5 hours) adds depth. For a large home or a short week, three 60 minute sessions (about 3 hours) do the job. All three keep one free day.

Key takeaways

  • Pick your schedule by the minutes you really have a day, not by the home you wish you had.
  • 15 minutes a day covers the basics of a small home. 30 minutes adds a little more depth. 60 minutes is for bigger homes or a shorter week.
  • Every version keeps one free day and a weekly bathroom and bedding job.
  • If you miss a day, skip it. Do not double up the next one.

How do I choose a weekly cleaning schedule?

The weekly cleaning schedule for busy people gives each day one focus area. This page does the opposite: you start from the time you have and pick the version that fits. Add the short daily routine (the bed, dishes and a 5 minute reset) to any of them. N.C. Cooperative Extension suggests splitting jobs into small doses and spacing them over the week rather than doing them all at once.

What is a 15 minute a day weekly cleaning schedule?

For a studio or one-bedroom home. About 1.5 hours of focus time a week.

Weekly cleaning schedule, 15 minutes a day
DayFocusMinutes
MondayBathroom: toilet, sink, mirror15
TuesdayVacuum the main living areas15
WednesdayKitchen: stove, microwave, sink15
ThursdayDust and wipe light switches and handles15
FridayFridge check and take out recycling10
SaturdayChange the sheets and start the laundry15
SundayFree day0
Total85

What is a 30 minute a day weekly cleaning schedule?

For a two-bedroom home or a couple. About 2.5 hours of focus time a week.

Weekly cleaning schedule, 30 minutes a day
DayFocusMinutes
MondayBathroom: full clean including shower or tub and floor30
TuesdayVacuum all rooms, shake out rugs25
WednesdayKitchen: appliances, cabinet fronts, mop30
ThursdayDust all rooms, wipe handles and switches20
FridayFridge clear-out, trash, recycling15
SaturdaySheets and towels, mop hard floors30
SundayFree day or one catch-up task0 to 15
Total150 to 165

What is a 60 minute a day weekly cleaning schedule?

For a larger home, a family or a week where you would rather clean for three days and be done. About 4 hours of focus time a week, in three longer sessions.

Weekly cleaning schedule, three 60 minute sessions
DayFocusMinutes
TuesdayBathrooms and kitchen60
ThursdayDust and vacuum every room60
SaturdayBedding, laundry, floors and the fridge60
Other daysDaily routine only0
Total180

Three long sessions are easier for some people and harder for others. If you tend to skip a long session, move to the 30 minute version.

Which weekly cleaning schedule should I pick?

  • Short on time or energy: the 15 minute version. It is better to keep a small schedule than to abandon a big one.
  • Normal week, shared home: the 30 minute version, with zones split between people.
  • Long workdays, free weekends: the 60 minute version, with weekdays on the daily routine only.
  • Not sure: start with 15 minutes for two weeks, then add time only where the home needs it.

Questions people ask

How long should a weekly cleaning take?

About 1.5 hours of focus time a week for a studio, 2.5 hours for a two-bedroom home and 4 hours for a larger home, on top of a short daily routine. Spread across the week that is 15 to 30 minutes a day.

Is 15 minutes a day of cleaning enough?

For a small home, yes, if you cover one area a day and keep the bathroom and bedding on a weekly rhythm. In a larger home or with children and pets, use the 30 minute version.

Should I clean a little every day or a lot once a week?

Both work. A little every day keeps the home steady and each session short. A longer weekly session leaves other days free. Choose the one you will actually keep.

What if I miss a day?

Skip it, or do it on your free day. Doubling up the next day is how schedules get abandoned.
